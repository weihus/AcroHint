Attention Is All You Need

Vaswani et al. · NeurIPS 2017 · (Simulated arXiv paper for demo purposes)

Abstract

The dominant sequence transduction models are based on complex recurrent or convolutional neural networks that include an encoder and a decoder. The best performing models also connect the encoder and decoder through an attention mechanism. We propose a new simple network architecture, the Transformer, based solely on attention mechanisms, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions entirely. Experiments on two machine translation (MT) tasks show these models to be superior in quality while being more parallelizable and requiring significantly less time to train.

1. Introduction

Recurrent neural networks (RNN), and in particular long short-term memory (LSTM) and gated recurrent unit (GRU) networks, have been firmly established as state-of-the-art approaches in sequence modeling and transduction problems such as language modeling and machine translation. Numerous efforts have since continued to push the boundaries of recurrent language models (RLM) and encoder-decoder architectures.

Attention mechanisms have become an integral part of compelling sequence modeling and transduction models. The self-attention mechanism (SAM) allows the model to draw global dependencies between input and output. In this work we use multi-head attention (MHA) to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different positions.

2. Background

The goal of reducing sequential computation also forms the foundation of the Extended Neural GPU (ENGPU), ByteNet, and ConvS2S, all of which use convolutional neural networks (CNN) as basic building block, computing hidden representations in parallel for all input and output positions. In these models, the number of operations required to relate signals from two arbitrary input or output positions grows in the distance between positions, linearly for ConvS2S and logarithmically for ByteNet. This makes it more difficult to learn dependencies between distant positions.

In the Transformer, the RNN and CNN computations are replaced entirely by self-attention. Each layer of MHA applies dot-product attention (DPA) scaled by the square root of the key dimension. The attention weight matrix is computed via a softmax over scaled dot-product attention (DPA) scores, which are then used to produce a weighted sum over value vectors.

3. Model Architecture

Most competitive neural sequence transduction models have an encoder-decoder structure. The encoder maps an input sequence of symbol representations to a sequence of continuous representations. Given these, the decoder then generates an output sequence of symbols one element at a time. The Transformer follows this overall architecture using stacked self-attention and point-wise, fully connected layers for both the encoder and decoder. Positional encoding (PE) is added to give the model information about the relative or absolute position of tokens.

We also employ residual connections (RC) around each of the two sub-layers, followed by layer normalization (LN). That is, the output of each sub-layer is LN(x + Sublayer(x)), where RC and LN are applied before the sub-layer input. To facilitate these RC, all sub-layers in the model, as well as the embedding layers, produce outputs of dimension d_model = 512.
